The real screen

  1. One key. The easiest.

    One recovery phrase and you are done. To start, this is all you need.

  2. Several keys. The safest.

    Several keys must agree to spend. Lose one? The bitcoin are not lost.

  3. Two of three.

    You pick the numbers. The wallet writes the rule as a sentence, and the rule is written into the script, not the app.

  4. Lose one? You can still spend.

    The other two are enough. The lock opens.

  5. One alone is not enough.

    No single key can spend by itself. The lock stays shut.

What kind of wallet?

Cancel

One key, several keys that must agree, or no keys at all

One key EasiestA single key signs every payment. Simplest to use and to back up: one recovery phrase and you are done.
Several keys must agree (multisig) SafestM-of-N: e.g. any 2 of 3 keys must approve. Losing one key does not lose the coins, and no single key can spend alone.
Custom rules (policy) Coming soonTimelocks, inheritance and recovery paths via Miniscript. Not available yet.
Somewhere else: just watch itFollow a wallet you already have, from its public key or its descriptor. Balances and history; cannot spend.

The real screen

  1. Under the hood.

    Nothing hidden between you and your keys. Look inside.

  2. Three layers. No secrets.

    The interface, the engine, the vault. Each with its real name.

  3. The engine.

    Rust. BDK for Bitcoin, LWK for Liquid, Miniscript for custom rules (coming). Public libraries, not a service.

  4. The vault.

    Recovery phrases sleep encrypted on your computer. A slow gear, on purpose. On a Mac, Touch ID unlocks it; auto-lock locks it again.

  5. Read the code.

    The whole wallet is open source. Read it, build it, check it.

The real screen

  1. The site proposes.

    A loan on Templar Protocol. A link opens the wallet: no extension, no key in the browser.

  2. The wallet reads for itself.

    On one side what the site says, on the other what the wallet reads from the transaction itself. They must agree.

  3. You sign.

    You confirm, you sign, the signature goes back to the site. The key never moved.

  4. No match, no signature.

    The site cannot change what the wallet sees. If one line is off, the button is not there.
